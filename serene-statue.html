Serene Statue

Photogrammetry · Nikon P510 → RealityScan → Blender → Unreal Engine 5 · Nanite and baked low-poly

At a Glance

A carved wooden bust that I photographed, rebuilt as a 3D model, cleaned up in Blender, and brought into Unreal Engine 5 as a Nanite mesh. This was a weekend project to learn the whole pipeline, from camera to engine.

In part 2, I baked the scan down to a game-ready low-poly version with its own textures, so the statue also works in projects that can't use Nanite.

Capture

I took close to 100 photos with a Nikon P510 in my greenhouse, where the plastic walls give soft, even light on overcast days. The statue sat on a turntable, and I shot three rings at different heights, turning it a little between each photo.

RealityScan

My first try wouldn't align. All the photos were taken from fixed positions and without the background spinning, RealityScan was unable to make any sense of the data. Masking out the background with the built-in AI masking tool managed to save my work and give me a useable scan.

Triangles and Nanite

The raw scan came out at about 2 million triangles: tons of detail and heavy. I simplified it to about 1 million, which kept the silhouette and the carved detail. Part 2 covers baking that detail into a much lighter version.

Cleanup in RealityScan and Blender

The raw scan included the cork base the statue sat on, so I did the first cleanup in RealityScan and removed the cork. That still left an uneven sliver under the base. In Blender I used Bisect with Clear Inner and Fill to cut the bottom flat and close it, moved the origin to the bottom so it sits properly on surfaces in engine, and exported it as an FBX.

In Unreal Engine

I imported the FBX with Nanite turned on and placed the statue in my scenes to see how it holds up next to other assets and lighting. The statue is the only asset of mine in these scenes; everything else is from Fab.

Part 2: Making It Game-Ready

Nanite handled the 1-million-triangle scan fine, but a lot of projects can't use Nanite. For part 2, I baked the scan down to a light, traditional game mesh in Blender 5.2, then put both versions side by side in Unreal Engine 5.8 to see if I could tell them apart. Up close, they look nearly identical.

Decimate and UVs

I duplicated the scan and used Blender's Decimate modifier to bring the copy down to about 10,000 triangles, checking in wireframe that the silhouette still held up. Then I unwrapped it, hiding the UV seams up the back and around the base, shrank the base's UV island since nobody sees it, and packed everything with a margin so the textures don't bleed across the seams.

Baking

Next I baked the detail from the full scan onto the low-poly: three 2K maps for normals, ambient occlusion and base colour. The normal map carries the carved detail, the AO map adds the shadows in the creases, and the base colour comes from the scan's own texture. I smooth-shaded the low-poly before baking so the normal map didn't pick up flat facets, and baked it in DirectX format so Unreal reads it without any extra settings.

Matching It in Unreal

I imported the low-poly without Nanite and built a master material that every asset in my planned nature pack can share. Each asset just gets its own material instance with its own textures. At first the low-poly looked paler than the scan. The textures matched, so I compared the materials and found that the one Unreal generated for the Nanite version had specular at 0 and roughness at 1. Matching those settings fixed it. As before, the statue is the only asset of mine in these scenes.

The side-by-side also showed a small gap under her chin. That's from the capture: the camera never saw that spot, which is why a lower ring of photos is first on my list below.

The Numbers

  • Triangles: 994,517 for the Nanite scan (down from 997,836 after cleanup in Blender), 9,946 for the low-poly (about 1%)
  • Mesh size on disk: about 10.27 MB vs 0.21 MB (about 2%, not counting textures)
  • Textures: 2K normal, AO and base colour maps

Why Bake When Nanite Exists?

Nanite's built-in fallback mesh, used on platforms that can't run Nanite, is only 2,308 triangles with no baked detail. Anyone shipping to mobile, or working in a project that doesn't use Nanite, needs a proper low-poly mesh.

Next, I'm scanning rocks, stumps and bark from local hikes, working toward a nature asset pack.

What I'd Do Differently

  • Add an even lower ring of photos, under the chin, to pick up the underside.
  • Put a plain backdrop behind the turntable, so there's less background for the software to sort out.
  • Get closer, so the statue fills more of each frame and every photo carries more detail.
